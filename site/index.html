You already know this part of the session

Five moments that eat real table time, every session, in every group.

"Who got the magic sword?"

Loot gets read off a list, everyone half-remembers who took what, and the DM ends up reconstructing the party's inventory from memory two sessions later.

"Wait, when did we last eat?"

Rations, torches, spell durations — the moment anyone tries to track them by hand is the moment the table stops playing and starts doing arithmetic.

"Let me check if I can craft..."

A five-minute rules lookup in the middle of a scene, just to find out if the party has the right components sitting in six different backpacks.

"What does the shop have?"

Customize shops so players know exactly what's available to buy — instead of the same generic stock in every town, or a DM improvising a list on the spot.

"How late is it?"

Keep track of in-game time as thoroughly as you need — down to the turn if a spell's about to run out, or just the day if that's all the table actually cares about.

Finally, An App That Gets How You Actually Play

Elemental Inventory handles the bookkeeping so you can focus on the storytelling — built by players who'd rather spend the minute on the adventure than the math.

Seven Things It Actually Does

Time Keeper

Your Digital Quartermaster

Advance time by turn, hour, or day, and every linked counter — rations, torches, a hireling's daily wage — ticks and updates on its own. What used to be a prolonged end-of-day group discussion is now one button.

Loot & Party Loot

30-Second Loot Distribution

The DM drops a haul in, players pick what they want, and anything nobody claims goes straight to a shared party pool instead of one character's overloaded pack. No more fifteen items and a scramble of handwritten notes.

Shop

A Storefront That Restocks Itself

Players buy against the current stock with your discounts and fees already applied — no more spot-pricing every item out loud mid-session. Auto-stocked by category and rarity, so the DM can stand up a brand-new town's shop in seconds instead of building a list from scratch.

Barter

Haggling Without the Spreadsheet

Trade item-for-item with players, NPCs, or merchants on a shared board — what you want on top, what you're offering underneath — with a live balance bar showing even, short, or surplus. No more arguing over what a magic sword is "worth" in torches.

Macros

A Dice Roller That Updates Your Sheet

Roll a macro against a DC and it applies the consequence itself — an item's quantity or a counter's value changes automatically on a pass or a fail, instead of everyone updating their own numbers by hand afterward.

Crafting

Your Personal Crafting Consultant

The app already knows what's sitting in your pack — it shows you what you can actually build right now, with rules that flex for 5e, homebrew, or a Fallout-style skill-check system.

Custom Inventory Sets

One App, Every Adventure

Upload your own catalog for a homebrew setting, and it slots in everywhere — inventory, shop, loot tables — right alongside the built-in D&D 5e SRD ruleset.

Give Your DM Superpowers

Run the table, not the paperwork

Stand up a shop pre-loaded with an appropriate stock in seconds, see every player's inventory at a glance, and drop in homebrew items without leaving the session.

A campaign that stays yours

Everything syncs peer-to-peer, device to device — there's no cloud database holding your campaign, and no reason a mid-session catalog update shouldn't reach every connected player immediately.

Encumbrance, gold, and every item your character is carrying, tracked automatically and visible at a glance.

The whole party, one picture

See what everyone's carrying without a single "does anyone have a torch?" — and keep playing when the wifi doesn't cooperate.
